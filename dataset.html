About the Survey & How It Works

This project explores how university students use and rely on generative AI tools (like ChatGPT) for their everyday schoolwork. A total of 2,613 university students answered 30 questions grouped into 6 simple sections (Sections A–F): 5 background questions about who they are, and 25 questions about their AI habits.

1. How Student Answers Are Scored (1 to 5 Scale)

For every question in Sections B through F, students picked how much they agreed on a simple 1 to 5 scale:

1 = Strongly Disagree “I never do this or don’t rely on AI this way at all.”

2 = Disagree “I rarely use AI for this part of my schoolwork.”

3 = Neutral “I’m in the middle—sometimes I do, sometimes I don’t.”

4 = Agree “I regularly rely on AI for this mental task.”

5 = Strongly Agree “I heavily depend on AI for this almost every time.”

  • Average Score (out of 5): Each section has 5 questions. We average a student’s 5 answers to get their overall score for that section (from 1.00 to 5.00).
  • What Counts as “High Reliance”? An average score of 3.50 or higher means the student mostly answered Agree or Strongly Agree.
  • % Who Agree: The percentage of students who picked either 4 (Agree) or 5 (Strongly Agree) on a question.

2. How the Survey Connects to the Brain (Sections B–F)

Each of the five AI-habit sections (Sections B–F) matches a specific part of the human brain that handles that exact type of thinking. Hover over or click any colored region on the brain—or any section card on the right—to see how they match up:

🧠 Color-Coded Brain Map Hover or click to highlight matching section
Section B: Everyday AI Usage ● Parietal Cortex (Tool Use)

Measures how often students use AI as an everyday tool to write assignments, plan study schedules, and finish homework faster.

Section C: Habit & Comfort Reliance ● Nucleus Accumbens (Habit & Relief)

Measures how much students feel they need AI to feel comfortable working, and whether they check AI before trying to think on their own.

Section D: Trust & Fact-Checking ● Anterior Cingulate (Error Detector)

Measures how much students trust AI answers and whether they double-check AI responses for mistakes before turning them in.

Section E: Deep & Critical Thinking ● Prefrontal Cortex (Deep Thinking)

Measures whether using AI causes students to analyze problems less, skip independent research, and do less deep thinking of their own.

Section F: Memory & Study Decisions ● Hippocampus (Memory & Planning)

Measures how much students rely on AI to pick research topics, decide what to study next, and guide their academic choices.

Section A asked 5 basic background questions about each student:

  1. Gender (Male or Female)
  2. Age (From 18 to 28 years old)
  3. University Type (Private University, Public University, or Other)
  4. Academic Level (Undergraduate or Postgraduate)
  5. Major / Department (Such as English, Computer Science (CSE), Business (BBA), Electrical Engineering (EEE), Economics, Law, Pharmacy, and Architecture)
